Generated by `scripts/measure_outcomes.py` from `data/layoffs.csv` (raw) and `outputs/layoffs_cleaned.csv` (export of `layoffs_staging2`).

Raw: 2,361 rows. Cleaned: 1,995 rows (366 removed, 15.5%).

Format fix (not counted above): 2,360 raw dates stored as m/d/Y text were converted to a DATE column (0 left as text).

Reconciliation: replaying the SQL rules in pandas gives 1,995 rows, which matches the cleaned export (1,995).

The one remaining missing industry is Bally's Interactive: it has no other row to copy an industry from, so it is left as NULL rather than guessed.

## Findings

- **Early-stage startups cut deeper.** Seed-Series B companies cut a median **30%** of staff vs **11%** for Post-IPO companies (n=371 vs n=309; Mann-Whitney U, one-sided p = 3.1e-41). **14.8%** of early-stage events were full shutdowns (100% laid off) vs **1.0%** for Post-IPO, about **15x** higher.
- **More funding, smaller cuts.** Funds raised vs % of staff laid off: Spearman rho = **-0.34** (n=1572, p = 1.8e-44).
- **Six months held over half the total.** Oct 2022-Mar 2023 accounts for **54%** of all dated layoffs (206,863 of 383,159).
- **A few large events dominate.** 65 events of 1,000+ people (**4%** of 1,617 events with a count) make up **48%** of all layoffs.

## Limits

- The source is media-reported layoffs, so small companies are under-counted.
- % laid off is missing for many rows, and small companies report it more often. The stage comparison only covers rows where it is reported.
- Totals are a floor: rows without a layoff count are excluded from sums.
